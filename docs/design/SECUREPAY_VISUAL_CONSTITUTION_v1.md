# SecurePay Visual Constitution v1

**Status:** LOCKED visual baseline from approved Batch 1 — extended through Batch 11 certification candidate
**Date:** 21 August 2026

## 1. Purpose

This constitution prevents SecurePay from becoming a collection of individually attractive screens that do not feel like one Market.

The governing product expression is:

> **The Market leads. SecurePay supports.**

SecurePay must feel present, trusted and capable, but it should not compete with the trader, the trade, the agreement or the action that needs attention.

The second governing expression is:

> **The SecurePay mark is the light. We do not draw a lighthouse around it.**

The official mark stays unchanged. Meaning comes from context, placement, restrained motion, halo and a small companion status badge.

---

## 2. Product truth before visual theatre

The interface may guide, explain, interpret and preview. It may not invent authoritative money or agreement truth.

Visual treatments must never independently assert:
- Payment Ready
- funded / unfunded truth
- release authority
- settlement completion
- balance
- rail eligibility
- quorum
- verification truth
- fees or quotes

When a surface is fixture-backed or illustrative, it must remain visibly a preview/demo and avoid language that could be mistaken for live financial authority.

---

## 3. Identity hierarchy

### 3.1 Full SecurePay wordmark
Use the official full wordmark only when SecurePay needs to sign or introduce the Market:
- signed-out Market entrance
- appropriate public/footer signature
- selected brand/knowledge contexts where the full identity genuinely helps

It must not be used simply to fill empty space.

### 3.2 Living SecurePay Mark
Use the official green icon for internal orientation and state communication:
- journey headers
- signed-in Market rooms
- identity doorway
- agreement workspaces
- money rooms
- review / issue states
- loading / waiting / completion states

The icon itself is never redrawn, recoloured, distorted, anthropomorphised or replaced by a lighthouse, street lamp, shield, mascot or another metaphor.

### 3.3 Presence levels
- **Polite:** normal orientation; the mark is present but not asking for attention.
- **Present:** SecurePay is actively listening, guiding or explaining.
- **Commanding:** rare moments where the user must notice a caution, confirmation or critical next step. Command comes from context, contrast and status treatment — not a giant logo.

---

## 4. Living SecurePay Mark vocabulary

The base mark remains green in every state.

| State | Meaning | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| resting | SecurePay is present | quiet halo / no active motion |
| listening | SecurePay is receiving the trader's intention | one restrained travelling/orbiting light |
| guiding | SecurePay is helping with the current step | slow soft breath / guide-light motion |
| success | a non-financial or backend-confirmed success | small green tick joins the mark |
| caution | user should inspect something | orange companion caution, gentle pulse |
| waiting | nothing is needed from the user right now | slow calm orbit |
| review | clarification or review is in progress | warm companion indicator, no alarm language |
| complete | an appropriate state is genuinely complete | settled green tick; motion stops |

`success` and `complete` must not be used as shortcuts for financial truth.

---

## 5. Market colour law

### Core palette
- SecurePay green: `#1f7a22`
- deep green: `#0f4f22`
- soft green: `#eaf5e7`
- SecurePay orange: `#ee7d1a`
- deep orange: `#c9570b`
- soft orange: `#fff3e8`
- warm cream: `#fffdf8`
- charcoal ink: `#171717`

### Use
**Green leads. Orange explains or calls attention. Cream gives the Market warmth.**

Orange is not a second dominant brand colour. It is the accent that tells the eye where to look.

Red is reserved for truly destructive/critical states and must not be used merely because a user has a question, review or disagreement.

---

## 6. Room energy law

Different Market rooms must not all have the same emotional volume.

| Room/state | Energy | User should feel |
|---|---|---|
| Market entrance | curious / welcoming | “I can do things here.” |
| creation journey | affirming / guided | “I am making this clearer.” |
| needs attention | alive / purposeful | “My Market needs me here.” |
| waiting | calm | “Nothing is wrong; I can carry on.” |
| completed | settled / satisfying | “That trade is safely in my record.” |
| money | precise / transparent | “I understand where things stand.” |
| review/issues | attentive / fair | “We can clarify this without panic.” |
| system failure | calm / explicit | “I know what happened and what to do.” |

The application must never feel like an exam room, bank back-office terminal or empty beige administration system.

---

## 7. Empty-wall rule

Whitespace is useful only when it gives the trader room to think. It must not make the Market feel abandoned.

Permitted atmosphere:
- faint SecurePay geometry
- very low-opacity guide marks
- restrained green/orange travelling-light traces
- small contextual story icons
- warm surface depth
- one encouraging line where it genuinely helps

Not permitted:
- literal Market illustrations as wallpaper
- decorative lighthouses/streetlights
- repeated giant logos
- unnecessary gradients behind every card
- constant motion
- random inspirational copy

---

## 8. Icon language

Every functional icon should primarily communicate one of five verbs:

1. **Meet** — people, agreement, handover, relationship
2. **Move** — goods, value, progress, delivery
3. **Grow** — community, referral, store, market history
4. **Confirm** — evidence, completion, acknowledgement
5. **Help** — guidance, clarification, support

Icons should tell small stories when motion is used. Motion must finish and rest.

Examples:
- work finishes, then value moves
- contributors converge on a purpose
- evidence joins the agreement line
- a condition is met, then the next stage becomes available

---

## 9. Motion law

Motion must communicate change, not decorate the screen.

### Timing
- fast UI response: ~180 ms
- state acknowledgement: ~300–700 ms
- calm breathing/guidance: ~3–4 s
- waiting orbit: ~8 s

### Never
- permanent bouncing
- flashing confirmation
- spinning logos that resemble loading when nothing is loading
- animation that implies money has moved

`prefers-reduced-motion` must suppress non-essential movement.

---

## 10. Trader language law

When explaining a state, prefer this order:

1. **What happened**
2. **What it means**
3. **What can I do next**

During creation, affirm progress without patronising the trader. Useful examples:
- “Good start — we already have the thread.”
- “You’re doing well. Keep going one clear choice at a time.”
- “Almost there — the important parts are taking shape.”

Where appropriate:

> “Answering these questions does not move money. We are only shaping the agreement.”

---

## 11. Signed-out versus signed-in law

### Signed out = possibility
The question is:

> **What could I do here?**

The entrance may be more theatrical and curiosity-led.

### Signed in = fluency in trade
The question is:

> **What is happening in my trade life right now?**

The signed-in Market should show action, waiting, movement and record — not marketing theatre.

---

## 12. Work & Trade and Life & Support

SecurePay acknowledges that the trader remains human inside the Market.

### Work & Trade
Examples:
- buy
- sell
- hire
- get paid
- supply
- build
- renovate
- security

### Life & Support
Examples:
- family
- contribute
- school
- help someone
- care split

Life & Support must not turn SecurePay into charity/NGO positioning. It is part of ordinary human economic life.

---

## 13. Typography hierarchy

- expressive serif may carry Market welcome / major human questions
- clean sans-serif carries operational information
- amounts must be immediately legible
- labels must never become microscopic to preserve a layout
- long Kenyan names, business names and KES amounts must wrap safely

Small meta labels may be visually quiet but must remain readable on 360 px phones.

---

## 14. Touch, focus and accessibility

- minimum interactive target: 44 px
- visible keyboard focus
- logical tab order
- no information conveyed by colour alone
- status companions require accessible labels where not decorative
- motion respects reduced-motion preference
- pages must tolerate 200% text where practicable

---

## 15. Responsive law

Canonical phone certification sizes:
- 360 × 800 — compact Android
- 412 × 915 — modern Android
- 390 × 844 — standard iPhone
- 430 × 932 — large iPhone

The phone experience is not a compressed desktop. It may change information order while preserving meaning.

For the Market entrance, the required mobile order is:

> Welcome → natural-language intention → compact SecurePay reaction → Work & Trade / Life & Support → familiar choices → full illustrative demo → deeper explanation.

The compact reaction must not push Work & Trade / Life & Support out of the immediate experience.

---

## 16. Market theme boundary

> **SecurePay does not wear costumes. The Market has seasons.**

Themes may later change:
- atmosphere
- background detail
- subtle patterns
- seasonal welcome accents

Themes may never change:
- official logo
- status meaning
- money-state meaning
- button meaning
- agreement truth
- accessibility

---

## 17. Approval and locking law

Every visual batch follows:

> Build → self-audit → experience → desktop review → Android review → iPhone review → correct → APPROVED → save reference → commit → LOCK.

A locked phase may be reopened only for:
- genuine usability failure
- accessibility defect
- backend/product-truth conflict
- technical bug

A later aesthetic preference alone is not sufficient to redesign a locked phase.
